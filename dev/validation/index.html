Validation

The package is held to three comparisons: the bitwise oracle against the reference implementation it replaced, the cross-code comparisons against RAPTOR and Blacklight, and the reproduction of published figures. The measurements, with their commands, are in the Verification record; the evidence files they cite (results/blp/) and the reference implementation live on the blp branch of the repository.

The oracle

The previous implementation is the reference: on identical decks the package reproduces its Stokes $I$ images on every path, its samplers, plasma decode, shadows and exact Monte Carlo sums bitwise on one hardware, and its $IQUV$ images, traces and torus Monte Carlo to round-off (bitwise under julia -C generic). On the A100 the reference device drivers and the package agree bitwise, $IQUV$ included. Across hardware a GPU pool against CPU threads differs by the two targets' floating-point contraction: $10^{-14}$–$10^{-9}$ of the image maximum on the oracle decks, $10^{-12}$–$3\times10^{-10}$ on the production decks, $10^{-16}$ relative on thin Monte Carlo sums. The test suite (14,453 tests, the structure gates included) passes on the CPU and on the A100.

Against RAPTOR

The reference implementation was compared with RAPTOR (Bronzwaer et al., 2018; Bronzwaer et al., 2020), an independent C code with its own geodesic integrator, polarized transfer step and camera, on analytic scenes and on binary-black-hole GRMHD snapshots; the package inherits that comparison through the oracle. RAPTOR ran from a fork that adds, each behind a compile-time switch that leaves the upstream build unchanged, the superposed Kerr–Schild metric, the step control $h = \min_A (r_A - r_{h,A})/C$ and termination, the plane-parallel camera, the AthenaK reader and plasma decode, the thermal coefficients of Marszewski et al. (2021), the IEEE/IAU readout and, for one leg, the exact constant-coefficient polarized step. Metrics are those of the EHT code comparisons (Gold et al., 2020).

With the exact step the two codes march the same states and differ only by the $O(h^4)$ truncation difference between RAPTOR's Christoffel form $(x^\mu, k^\mu)$ and the Hamiltonian form $(x^\mu, p_\mu)$ of the same RK4 march. With RAPTOR's native step the polarized differences are the discretisation error of that step, whose implicit trapezoid on absorbing segments converges at second order and loses phase with the Faraday depth. The thresholds of the binary images ($|\Delta F/F| \le 0.01$, $L_1(I) \le 0.03$, ${\rm NMSE}(I) \le 10^{-3}$, $|\Delta m| \le 0.01$, $|\Delta v_{\rm net}| \le 0.002$) are met on every snapshot; the net EVPA of the edge-on views crosses Faraday depths of $10^2$–$10^4$ rad and is not converged in $C$ in either code.

Against Blacklight

The figure reproductions below image the AthenaK single-hole torus with the package and with Blacklight (White, 2022) (its C++ code, with the dumps converted to its reader's layout) on matched cameras, plasma prescriptions and step controls. Stokes $I$ agrees to ${\rm NMSE}$ $7.9\times10^{-4}$ (the Mahakala setup, $256^2$, $R_{\rm high} = 10$) and $5.7\times10^{-3}$ (the KPolaris setup, $i = 60^\circ$, 230 GHz), with the flux within 0.35%; the residual is unchanged under step refinement and under sub-pixel shifts or rescaling of either image, so it is a sampling and plasma-decode difference between the codes, not integration or registration. Against the released Blacklight, $Q$ and $U$ agreed only where Faraday effects are weak and Stokes $V$ differed by a factor 2–7 at every frequency. The cause is in Blacklight: its unsplit polarized coupling (polarized.cpp, the path taken when absorption and rotation coexist) writes the $(U,V)$ entries of the Landi Degl'Innocenti matrices $M_2$ and $M_3$ into the $(Q,U)$ slot, so the $U \leftrightarrow V$ coupling of Faraday conversion is lost; upstream master carries the same lines. Both codes evaluate the same coefficient fits, thresholds, pitch-angle sign and transfer matrix, and the package's segment operator equals $\exp(-K\,ds)$ to $1.6\times10^{-10}$ on 4000 random admissible coefficient sets. With the two indices corrected in Blacklight and nothing else, the images agree: at 230 GHz NMSE$(V)$ falls from 52 to 0.041 (correlation 0.13 to 0.98) and NMSE$(Q,U)$ from 0.37/0.31 to 0.012/0.011, at 1 THz NMSE$(V)$ from 4.6 to 0.076, with $I$ unchanged. The patch, the builds and the statistics are under results/blp/stokes_v/ on the blp branch.

Published figures

The generators under figures/ reproduce published figures with the package; each directory holds a README with a standing table (one row per figure: what the paper shows, what was traced and with which data, the measured numbers, and a tag). The images and run records are on the blp branch under results/blp/. The data substitutions are stated in each table: none of the papers' GRMHD simulations is available on the host, so every GRMHD figure uses the AthenaK single-hole torus series (spin 0.9, 819 dumps 30 M apart).
