Method

BlackLight+ (the package BlackLightPlus) is a Julia package for general-relativistic ray tracing and fully polarized radiative transfer in arbitrary spacetimes, on CPUs and GPUs, with end-to-end automatic differentiation (AD). It images GRMHD simulation data or semi-analytic sources, and includes a Monte Carlo Compton-scattering module for spectral synthesis. The design follows two reference lineages — Blacklight (White, 2022) for structure and ipole (Mościbrodzka and Gammie, 2018) for the polarized transfer scheme — with the analytic-Kerr machinery of AART (Cárdenas-Avendaño et al., 2023) and Krang.jl (Chang, 2024), and a grmonty-class Monte Carlo (Dolence et al., 2009).

Two design rules hold throughout. Every physical or geometric primitive has a single implementation shared by all consumers (CPU imaging, GPU kernels, Monte Carlo, AD). Everything reachable from a march loop is pure, allocation-free, isbits, and StaticArrays-typed, so one code path serves CPU threads, KernelAbstractions GPU kernels, and ForwardDiff/Enzyme differentiation.

Conventions

Metric signature $(-,+,+,+)$; geometrized units $G=c=M=1$ (lengths in $r_g=GM/c^2$, times in $r_g/c$); Cartesian Kerr–Schild (CKS) coordinates $x^\mu=(t,x,y,z)$ with $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+f\,l_\mu l_\nu$. The geodesic state is the 8-vector $y=[x^\mu;\,p_\mu]$ carrying covariant momentum. Transfer coefficients propagate in Lorentz-invariant form: $j_\nu/\nu^2$, $\alpha_\nu\,\nu$, $\rho_\nu\,\nu$, and invariant intensity $\mathcal I=I_\nu/\nu^3$; the camera output is $S_\nu=\nu_{\rm obs}^3\,\mathcal S$. All physics arithmetic is Float64 unless a Float32 geodesic lane or MixedLanes is requested; plasma and radiation quantities are in cgs, with the code magnetic field $b^\mu$ in Heaviside–Lorentz units ($B=\sqrt{b^2}\,c\sqrt{4\pi\rho_{\rm unit}}$, $\beta=2p_{\rm gas}/b^2$, $\sigma=b^2/\rho$).

Spacetimes

Every spacetime supplies only a covariant metric closure $x\mapsto g_{\mu\nu}(x)$; all derivative machinery (inverse metric gradients, Christoffel symbols) is derived by forward-mode AD (ForwardDiff), so a new metric never needs hand-coded derivatives.

  • KerrSchildCartesian(a) — Kerr in CKS form $g_{ab}=\eta_{ab}+f\,l_a l_b$, with the closed-form inverse $g^{ab}=\eta^{ab}-f\,l^a l^b$ (exact because $l$ is null), so no matrix inverse is needed on the hot path.
  • UserMetric(f) — wraps any closure $x\mapsto g_{\mu\nu}$; inverse and gradients come from StaticArrays inv and AD. Minkowski() is flat space.
  • SuperposedKerrSchild(masses, spins, centers) — superposes $N$ Kerr–Schild holes, $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\sum_A f_A l^A_\mu l^A_\nu$, an analytic approximate binary (or $N$-body) spacetime. The cross terms break the single-hole inverse identity; the inverse follows from the Woodbury identity $g^{-1}=\eta^{-1}-U(\mathbf 1_N+FU^{\mathsf T}L)^{-1}FU^{\mathsf T}$, with $L$ the $4\times N$ matrix of the $l^A_\mu$, $U=\eta^{-1}L$ and $F={\rm diag}(f_A)$, so a binary needs one $2\times2$ solve, and AD differentiates through it. The construction reduces bitwise to single Kerr when a second mass vanishes; rays end by the shared rule of March termination.
  • BoostedSuperposedKerrSchild — the superposition in motion: each hole's term in the rest frame of its instantaneous velocity along a prescribed trajectory (CircularOrbit or a closure), the covector boosted back, the Woodbury inverse unchanged; a non-stationary spacetime whose geodesic flow keeps $\partial_t g$. Capture is per hole at the rest-frame radius $r'_A < r_{h,A} + \epsilon$ or by the redshift cut.
  • ADMGrid — a frozen-slice numerical spacetime: a Cartesian grid of the ten ADM fields $(\alpha,\beta^i,\gamma_{ij})$, reassembled into the 4-metric via the standard 3+1 decomposition. Fields are interpolated with a $C^1$ cubic Catmull–Rom tensor-product stencil — $C^1$ continuity is required because AD differentiates through the interpolant to obtain $\partial g_{\mu\nu}$, and a trilinear ($C^0$) interpolant would produce discontinuous Christoffels at cell faces. Rays terminate where the lapse falls below a threshold (numerical horizon) or off-grid. adm_grid_from_metric samples any analytic metric onto a grid for testing.
  • Z4c input — read_athenak_bin parses AthenaK (Stone et al., 2026) v1.1 .bin AMR dumps, as written by its numerical-relativity solver (Zhu et al., 2025); resample_z4c_adm / z4c_grid_spacetime map the conformal Z4c variables to physical ADM ($\gamma_{ij}=\tilde g_{ij}/\chi$, $\alpha$, $\beta^i$ passed through), sampled on blocks extended by ghost layers (Sampling the meshblock hierarchy), and build an ADMGrid. A real binary-black-hole merger dump reconstructs with $g\,g^{-1}=\mathbb 1$ to $4\times10^{-16}$ and lenses into two puncture shadows. The frozen slice sets $\partial_t g=0$, valid while the light-crossing time of the imaged volume is short compared to the spacetime's evolution timescale.

Geodesics

Null geodesics integrate the super-Hamiltonian $H=\tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu$:

\[\frac{dx^\mu}{d\lambda}=g^{\mu\nu}p_\nu,\qquad \frac{dp_\mu}{d\lambda}=-\tfrac12\,(\partial_\mu g^{\alpha\beta})\,p_\alpha p_\beta .\]

For stationary metrics $\dot p_t\equiv0$, so integrating covariant momentum conserves the energy $E=-p_t$ exactly (zero drift by construction); only the three spatial $p_i$ are advanced, with $\partial_i g^{\mu\nu}$ from AD. nullify_covariant closes the initial condition by solving the null quadratic for the root with $p_t<0$ (positive energy).

Two integration modes are provided. trace_geodesic is an adaptive high-order solve (Vern7, reltol $10^{-9}$–$10^{-10}$) with event-terminated horizon capture and escape. The imaging products march with fixed-step classical RK4 (rk4_step) at the control step $h=(r-r_h)/C$ of march_control (dense near the hole), which doubles as the radiation sampling grid; trace returns these trajectories ordered source to camera with their affine steps. Validation gates: exact $p_t$ conservation, $L_z$ and null drift below $10^{-8}$, Schwarzschild shadow $b_c=3\sqrt3$ to better than $10^{-3}$.

PlaneParallelCamera places a parallel-ray image plane at distance $d$ and inclination $i$. Each pixel's ray is the photon received at the camera, traced backward with the orientation and normalization of Conventions, so the fluid-frame frequency needs no per-pixel factor. The pixel-to-ray map camera_ray is one isbits function shared by the host camera_rays and the device ray generator, so host and device rays are identical.

March termination

Every fixed-control march, on threads or on a device, unpolarized or polarized, in Float64 or in the Float32 lane, ends through one rule, Spacetime.march_stop (src/physics/Spacetime/termination.jl), so CPU and device integrate the same sample set:

  • Horizon backstop: $d<\epsilon_h=10^{-3}$ (HORIZON_EPS_SAMPLED), where $d=r-r_h$ for Kerr, $d=\min_A(r_A-r_{h,A})$ for a superposed binary, and the lapse excess over its floor for an ADM grid; a non-finite $d$ also captures.
  • Redshift cut: the photon energy in the local normal frame, $E_n=-k\cdot n=\alpha\,|dx^t/d\lambda|$ (camera energy 1), exceeds $E_c=10^2$ (CAPTURE_REDSHIFT).
  • Escape: outbound ($R>R_{\rm prev}$) beyond the escape sphere ($1.1\times10^3$ M; smaller for an ADM grid) or beyond the emission radius.

A backward ray falling toward a hole approaches the future horizon asymptotically: $x^t\to-\infty$ while $E_n\propto1/(r-r_h)$ grows without bound. The redshift cut ends it at finite affine parameter. The affine length left beyond the cut is $\Delta\lambda_{\rm rest}\approx\alpha_h/(\kappa E_c)$ ($\kappa$ the surface gravity, $\alpha_h\le1/\sqrt2$ the Kerr–Schild lapse on the horizon; $0.045\,M$ at $a=0.9$), and since the fluid-frame frequency there is at least $E_c\,\nu_{\rm obs}/(2\gamma)$, the omitted observed intensity obeys

\[\Delta I_\nu\le\nu_{\rm obs}^3\,\frac{L_{\rm unit}}{\nu_{\rm obs}}\, \Delta\lambda_{\rm rest}\max_{\rm beyond\ cut}\frac{j_\nu}{\nu^2},\]

with transmission bounded by one. test/physics/spacetime_termination.jl checks this bound ray by ray and $\Delta\lambda_{\rm rest}$ against $\alpha_h/(\kappa E_c)$ (measured maximum 0.0450 against 0.0466 on a plunging Kerr deck). Up to the cut the parallel-transported camera frame of the polarized march keeps the invariant $-k\cdot e_u$ within 1% of its camera value (maximum drift $5.2\times10^{-3}$ in Float64, $6.0\times10^{-3}$ in Float32). In a superposed binary the companion's potential moves each hole's horizon outward (to $r_A\approx1.04$ for $m_A=0.5$ at separation 25), where $r_A-r_{h,A}$ stalls above $\epsilon_h$; the redshift cut is what captures those rays (test/physics/spacetime_superposed.jl). The moving binary BoostedSuperposedKerrSchild applies the same rule to each hole's rest-frame radius at the ray's time.

The precise march

The precise mode (Plans.precise, guide Precise ray tracing) keeps the fixed-control structure of the march, whose step $h$ is a function of the state alone, and replaces the parts whose errors set the floor of the production march against the closed form.

Ninth-order step. Verner9 is the ninth-order solution of Verner's 9(8) pair (Verner, 2010): fifteen stages, local error $O(h^{10})$, so at the control step $h=d/C$ the image error falls as $C^{-9}$. The embedded eighth-order weights are not used: an accept/reject decision would make the step sequence a discontinuous function of the initial state, while with $h=h(y)$ two backends whose arithmetic differs in the last bit take the same steps and end within that rounding times the ray's condition number. On a long march the rounding of the state update $y\leftarrow y+\Delta y$ accumulates once per step; march_step_compensated carries the state as the unevaluated sum $y+y_{\rm lo}$, with $y_{\rm lo}$ the two-sum error of each update, so the update rounds once per ray.

Winding near a spinning horizon. A ray traced backward toward a black hole of spin $a$ approaches the past horizon, where its Kerr–Schild azimuth turns by $d\phi=2a\,dr/\Delta$, $\Delta=(r-r_+)(r-r_-)$. A step of radial extent $(r-r_+)/C$ turns it by $2|a|/((r-r_-)C)$, up to $7/C$ radians at $a=0.99$, and the ninth-order step loses its accuracy there. With winding the step distance is $d\,w$, $w=(1+q^4)^{-1/4}$, $q=2|a|/(r-r_-)$ (winding_factor, $r_-=a^2/r_+$), which bounds the turn per step by about $1/C$ and leaves the step unchanged where $r-r_-\gg2|a|$; a binary takes the smallest $d_A w_A$ over its holes.

Surface crossings at full order. An emission surface $s(x)=0$ (the equator $z=0$ for a thin disk, EquatorialPlane) is met inside the step whose end has the other sign of $s$. The crossing is the root in $c\in(0,h)$ of $s(\Phi_c(y))$, with $\Phi_c$ the integrator's own step of length $c$, found by Newton's iteration with $ds/dc=\nabla s\cdot dx/d\lambda$ at $\Phi_c(y)$ and a bisection fallback inside the bracket (surface_crossing). Since $\Phi_c$ is the method applied over $c\le h$, the crossing state carries the local error of the step, $O(h^{p+1})$; the root of the linear interpolant of $s$ between the step's ends would carry $O(h^2)$. The iteration converges in about one step per crossing from the secant start, so a crossing costs one or two partial steps.

Camera at infinity. A plane-parallel ray started at distance $d$ with no transverse momentum is not the ray an observer at infinity receives at the same screen point: the far zone bends the latter by $\delta p_\perp\sim Mb/d^2$ and displaces it by $\delta w\sim Mb/d$ at the plane, so its conserved quantities carry an error $O(M/d)$; at large $d$ the transverse offset $b$ is rounded against $d$, which leaves $\epsilon d/b$. The camera at infinity (infinity_ray) integrates the far zone in $u=1/s$, $s=x\cdot\hat n$, with the position $x=\hat n s+w$ and the momentum $p=(1,-\hat n+\delta p)$:

\[\frac{dw}{du}=-\frac{v_\perp}{u^2v_s},\qquad \frac{d\,\delta p}{du}=-\frac{F}{u^2v_s},\qquad \frac{d\tau}{du}=-\frac{v^t-v_s}{u^2v_s},\]

\[v=g^{\mu\nu}p_\nu\]

, $v_s=v\cdot\hat n$, $v_\perp=v-\hat n v_s$, $F=dp/d\lambda$, $\tau=t-s$. A metric with $g-\eta=O(M/r)$ gives $v_\perp=O(u^2)$, $F_\perp=O(u^3)$ and $F_\parallel=O(u^2)$, so $w$ and $\delta p$ are regular at $u=0$, where the ray from infinity has $w=(-\beta\cos i,\alpha,\beta\sin i)$ and $\delta p=0$; the Shapiro term $A/u$ of $d\tau/du$ is common to all screen points and is integrated apart. The offset $v_c=(g^{\mu\nu}-\eta^{\mu\nu})p_\nu$ (raise_index_offset) enters $v_\perp=(\delta p+v_c)_\perp$; Kerr–Schild spacetimes form it from their terms, $v_c=-f\,(l\cdot p)\,l$ for one black hole and the Woodbury correction for a superposition, so every term carries rounding of itself and never of $s$. The general solution also holds the mode $w\propto1/u$ of a ray at another angle, which an error of $\delta p_\perp$ excites with weight $1/u$ at the plane; the march therefore starts at $u_0=10^{-8}$ from the leading behaviour, $\delta p_\perp\propto u^2$ (trapezoid) and $w-w(0)\propto u$ (midpoint, with corrector passes), an error $O(u_0^2)$, and takes Verner9 steps $u\to2u$ to $u=1/d$. Each step $[u,2u]$ has the same relative structure, so its truncation is a fixed fraction of the bending in that step, below $10^{-15}\,M$ in total. On Kerr the started ray carries Bardeen's $\lambda=-\alpha\sin i$ and $\eta=(\alpha^2-a^2)\cos^2i+\beta^2$ to $3\times10^{-14}$ relative (test/physics/spacetime_integrators.jl).

Capture. In Kerr–Schild coordinates every ray that falls in approaches the past horizon with $E_n\propto1/(r-r_+)$ (the March termination rule), so the redshift cut alone captures and the precise mode sets $\epsilon_h=0$. A thin disk's observed intensity is $f(r)\,g^3$ with $g\le2\gamma/E_c$ beyond the cut, $\gamma$ the Lorentz factor of the gas relative to the normal observer, so the omitted intensity relative to the image maximum is at most $(2\gamma/E_c)^3 f_{\max}/I_{\max}$; at $E_c=10^6$ this is below $10^{-15}$. A higher cut costs $C\ln10/w$ steps per decade on captured rays only. On a superposed binary the horizons are approximate: $d$ stalls above zero while $E_n$ grows, and a march continued beyond $E_n\approx10^2$ loses the ray, so march_control keeps the cut there at most at CAPTURE_REDSHIFT whatever the budget.

A volumetric render samples its plasma once per step, so its accuracy is set by $C$ and not by the integrator (a torus image's flux converges as $C^{-2}$ with either); the precise mode serves the products whose accuracy is that of the geodesics: surface images (render_crossings), trajectories, shadows and capture maps.

Analytic Kerr fast path and lensing bands

For sources on surfaces in Kerr, an exact closed-form tracer (no ODE) inverts the geodesic with Legendre/Jacobi elliptic integrals in the formulation of Gralla and Lupsasca (2020) and its lensing companion (Gralla and Lupsasca, 2020), following the conventions of Krang.jl (Chang, 2024) and the finite-observer photon-ring machinery of AART (Cárdenas-Avendaño et al., 2023): conserved quantities $(\lambda,\eta)$ from Bardeen screen coordinates, quartic radial roots, angular and radial Mino-time integrals, and per-image-order landing points $(r_s,\phi_s,t_s)$. critical_curve returns the photon-shell boundary; lensing_band and band_grid build the nested order-$n$ lensing-band masks used by photon_ring_layers to compute razor-thin photon-ring images and, via the interferometry utilities, visibility amplitudes. The analytic $r_s$ agrees with the ODE tracer's equatorial crossing to $6\times10^{-6}$, and ForwardDiff propagates $dr_s/d(a,\theta_o)$ through the elliptic functions (verified against finite differences).

Surfaces at any polar angle

With $u=\cos^2\theta$ the polar potential factors as $\Theta\sin^2\theta=a^2(u_+-u)(u-u_-)$. For an ordinary ray ($\eta>0$, $u_-<0<u_+$) the antiderivatives $\Psi_\theta=\frac{F(\varphi|k)}{a\sqrt{-u_-}},\quad \Psi_\phi=\frac{\Pi(u_+;\varphi|k)}{a\sqrt{-u_-}},\quad \Psi_t=-\frac{2u_+}{a\sqrt{-u_-}}\,\frac{E(\varphi|k)-F(\varphi|k)}{2k},$ with $\sin\varphi=\cos\theta/\sqrt{u_+}$ and $k=u_+/u_-$, satisfy $d\Psi_\theta/d\theta=-1/\sqrt\Theta$, $d\Psi_\phi/d\theta=-1/(\sin^2\theta\sqrt\Theta)$ and $d\Psi_t/d\theta=-\cos^2\theta/\sqrt\Theta$, and $\Psi$ runs monotonically between the turning values $\pm\hat G/2$, $\hat G=2K(k)/(a\sqrt{-u_-})$. A ray traced back from the observer starts toward the pole when $\beta>0$ ($\theta_o<\pi/2$), so with $s_\beta={\rm sign}\,\beta$ (${\rm sign}\,0=+1$) it meets the surface $\theta=\theta_s$ after $m$ polar turning points at $G_i(m)=m\,\hat G_i-s_\beta\Psi_i(\theta_o)+\nu_\theta\Psi_i(\theta_s),\qquad \nu_\theta=s_\beta(-1)^m,\qquad i\in\{\theta,\phi,t\},$ where $\nu_\theta$ is the sign of $p^\theta$ of the photon at emission. A crossing exists when $\cos^2\theta_s<u_+$ and $G_\theta(m)>0$; at the equator $\Psi(\pi/2)=0$ and $m=n+H(\beta)$. A vortical ray ($\eta<0$, $0<u_-<u_+$) stays in one hemisphere; with $\sin^2\psi=(\cos^2\theta-u_-)/(u_+-u_-)$ and $k_v=1-u_+/u_-$, $\Psi_\theta=F(\psi|k_v)/(a\sqrt{u_-})$, $\Psi_\phi=\Pi(n_v;\psi|k_v)/((1-u_-)a\sqrt{u_-})$ with $n_v=(u_+-u_-)/(1-u_-)$, $\Psi_t=\sqrt{u_-}E(\psi|k_v)/a$, and the same formula holds with $\hat G$ the half oscillation $K(k_v)/(a\sqrt{u_-})$ and $\Psi$ centred on it. The radial inversion of $G_\theta$ is the sn, cn or sc function of cases II, III and IV (Gralla and Lupsasca (2020) App. B); case IV (no real root) is met by vortical rays only, since $R(0)=-a^2\eta<0$ forces two real roots when $\eta>0$.

Image orders follow Krang's labelling with every crossing in exactly one order. Let $m_0=0$ when the first leg of the traced ray reaches the surface and $m_0=1$ otherwise. A surface at most as inclined as the line of sight ($|\cos\theta_s|\le|\cos\theta_o|$, the equator among them) has the single crossing $m=n+m_0$ in order $n$; a surface closer to a pole has the pair $\{n,n+1\}$ when $n\equiv m_0\pmod 2$ (the near and far sides of a jet cone) and none otherwise; a vortical ray has the pair $\{2n+m_0,2n+m_0+1\}$. surface_prep holds the per-pixel state, surface_landing the crossing after $m$ turning points and image_crossings the crossings of order $n$. At the equator the surface path reproduces band_landing bit for bit.

Observer at infinity

pixel_prep and surface_prep take ro = Inf for an observer at infinity: $I_0$, $I_\pm$ and the azimuth converge, while $dt/dr=1+2/r+O(r^{-2})$ makes $I_1\sim\ln r$ and $I_2\sim r$ diverge. The landing time is then the regularized $\tilde t=\lim_{r_o\to\infty}(t_s+r_o+2\ln r_o)$, the emission time relative to the observer's retarded time, evaluated in closed form by the connection formula $\Pi(n;\varphi|k)+\Pi(k/n;\varphi|k)=F(\varphi|k)+\sqrt c\,R_C\big((c-1)(c-k),(c-n)(c-k/n)\big)$, $c=\csc^2\varphi$, whose $R_C$ term carries the logarithm. The finite-observer landings approach it as $1/r_o$ in all three radial cases.

Photon-ring orders

A ray that lands near the critical curve circles the photon shell before leaving: at screen distance $d$ from the critical point of shell radius $\tilde r$, successive images of one source radius satisfy $d_{m+1}/d_m\to e^{-\gamma}$, with azimuth lapse $\delta$ and time lapse $\tau$ per half orbit (Gralla and Lupsasca (2020) Eqs. 68, 70, 72; Johnson et al. (2020)), $\gamma=\frac{4\tilde r\sqrt{\tilde\chi}}{a\sqrt{-\tilde u_-}}K,\quad \delta=\frac{2}{\sqrt{-\tilde u_-}}\left[\frac{\tilde r+1}{\tilde r-1}K+\frac{\tilde\lambda}{a}\Pi\right]+2\pi H(\tilde r-\tilde r_0),\quad \tau=\frac{2}{a\sqrt{-\tilde u_-}}\left[\tilde r^2\frac{\tilde r+3}{\tilde r-1}K-2a^2\tilde u_+\frac{E-K}{2k}\right],$ $\tilde\chi=1-\Delta(\tilde r)/(\tilde r(\tilde r-1)^2)$, complete integrals of parameter $k=\tilde u_+/\tilde u_-$ (photon_shell_exponents; critical_point gives the screen point of $\tilde r$). The landings of orders 0–7 reproduce them: along the screen ray through a critical point, $\ln(d_{n-1}/d_n)-\gamma$, the azimuth step minus $2\pi-\delta$ and the time step minus $\tau$ fall as $e^{-\gamma n}$ to $10^{-6}$–$10^{-5}$ at $n=7$. The case-II time keeps that accuracy because $\sqrt{R}/(r-r_3)$ is evaluated from the factored potential, exactly zero at the turning point (the expanded polynomial leaves a residue there that $\sqrt{\cdot}$ lifts to $\sim\sqrt\varepsilon$ and $1/(r_4-r_3)$ amplifies near the critical curve); every term is evaluated at the inverted radius, so time and azimuth belong to the point of the geodesic at that radius. What remains is the conditioning of the near-double roots $r_3\approx r_4$; the landing radius agrees with Krang's to within $\varepsilon\,|\partial\ln r_s/\partial\ln s|$ along the screen ray at every order. lensing_band finds the band edges of any order by a logarithmic search toward the critical curve.

Jacobi functions near $m=1$

The radial inversion evaluates sn and cn (cases II and III) and sc (case IV) of the radial parameter $k_r$ at an argument $u$ linear in $G_\theta$. Near the critical curve the roots $r_3$, $r_4$ approach a double root, $m_1=1-k_r\to0$, and the rays that circle the photon shell carry $u$ to $K(k_r)\approx\ln(4/\sqrt{m_1})$ and beyond. jacobi_amplitude computes $\varphi=\mathrm{am}(u|m)$ by the arithmetic–geometric mean (A&S 16.4): from $(a_0,b_0,c_0)=(1,\sqrt{m_1},\sqrt m)$, $a_{j+1}=(a_j+b_j)/2$, $b_{j+1}=\sqrt{a_jb_j}$, $c_{j+1}=(a_j-b_j)/2$ until $c_N\le\varepsilon$, then $\varphi_N=2^Na_Nu$ and $\varphi_{j-1}=\tfrac12[\varphi_j+\arcsin(c_j\sin\varphi_j/a_j)]$. Two expansions replace it where they are exact to rounding: $\mathrm{am}=u-\tfrac14m(u-\tfrac12\sin2u)$ for $m<\sqrt\varepsilon$ (A&S 16.13.4), and $\mathrm{am}(u|m)=\mathrm{gd}\,u+\tfrac14m_1(\sinh u\cosh u-u)\,\mathrm{sech}\,u$ (A&S 16.15.4) only where $m_1\cosh^2u<\sqrt\varepsilon$. The m → 1 expansion is the first term of a series in $m_1\cosh^2u$, so its remainder is below $\varepsilon$ there; selected by $m_1<\sqrt\varepsilon$ alone it fails where it matters, since $m_1\cosh^2K\approx4$ whatever the size of $m_1$. jacobi_sncndn returns $\mathrm{sn}=\sin\varphi$, $\mathrm{cn}=\cos\varphi$ and $\mathrm{dn}=\sqrt{m_1+m\,\mathrm{cn}^2}$, the form of $\sqrt{1-m\,\mathrm{sn}^2}$ without cancellation as $\mathrm{sn}\to1$. Over $m_1\in[10^{-15},10^{-8}]$ and $0\le u\le2K$ the three functions agree with 50-digit values to $1.2\times10^{-13}$, and on the radial cuts of the code comparison, where $m_1$ reaches $10^{-15}$, the landing radius of order 0 agrees with a 40-digit landing to $8\times10^{-14}$. Wherever the m → 1 expansion is not taken the amplitude is the AGM's, bit for bit that of JacobiElliptic.jl.

Time-dependent sources

The landing time $t_s$ (zero at the observation, or the retarded time for an observer at infinity) makes a source $f(t,x,y)$ on the equator observable at time $t_{\rm obs}$ as $I=f(t_s+t_{\rm obs},r_s\cos\phi_s,r_s\sin\phi_s)\,g^p$. analytic_movie computes the landings of every pixel and order once and each frame as one pass over them; the default EquatorialSpot profile is the Gaussian of AART's flare model on a circular orbit, $f=\exp(-|x-x_c(t)|^2/w^2)$, $x_c=r_c(\cos(\Omega t+\phi_0),\sin(\Omega t+\phi_0))$.

Polarization by the Walker–Penrose constant

At a landing the photon momentum is $p_\mu=(-1,\nu_r\sqrt R/\Delta,\nu_\theta\sqrt{\Theta},\lambda)$. The emitter's rest frame is the zero-angular-momentum frame $e_{(t)}=(\partial_t+\omega\partial_\phi)/\alpha_L$, $e_{(r)}=\sqrt{\Delta/\Sigma}\,\partial_r$, $e_{(\phi)}=\sqrt{\Sigma/\mathcal A}\,\partial_\phi/\sin\theta$, $e_{(z)}=-\partial_\theta/\sqrt\Sigma$ ($\alpha_L=\sqrt{\Sigma\Delta/\mathcal A}$, $\omega=2ar/\mathcal A$) boosted by the emitter's velocity; there $p=\omega_{\rm em}(1,\hat k)$, the redshift is $g=1/\omega_{\rm em}$, and the synchrotron electric vector is $f=(0,\hat k\times b)$ for the rest-frame field $b$ (Narayan et al., 2021). For a field given as a spacetime vector $B^\mu$ the three conditions $f\cdot p=f\cdot u=f\cdot B=0$ of Cárdenas-Avendaño et al. (2023) select the same direction. The Walker–Penrose constant $\kappa=(r-ia\cos\theta)(\mathcal A_\kappa-i\mathcal B_\kappa),\quad \mathcal A_\kappa=(p^tf^r-p^rf^t)+a\sin^2\theta(p^rf^\phi-p^\phi f^r),\quad \mathcal B_\kappa=[(r^2+a^2)(p^\phi f^\theta-p^\theta f^\phi)-a(p^tf^\theta-p^\theta f^t)]\sin\theta$ is conserved along the ray, invariant under $f\to f+cp$, and $|\kappa|^2=(f\cdot f)(\eta+(\lambda-a)^2)$ (Himwich et al., 2020). On the screen, with $\nu=-(\alpha+a\sin\theta_o)$ and $\nu^2+\beta^2=\eta+(\lambda-a)^2$, $E_\alpha=(\beta\kappa_2-\nu\kappa_1)/(\nu^2+\beta^2)$ and $E_\beta=(\beta\kappa_1+\nu\kappa_2)/(\nu^2+\beta^2)$ (Gelles et al., 2021), so $E_\alpha^2+E_\beta^2=f\cdot f$; with north $+\beta$ and east $-\alpha$, $Q=E_\beta^2-E_\alpha^2$ and $U=-2E_\alpha E_\beta$ scaled to the polarized intensity. SynchrotronSurface is the power-law synchrotron surface of Narayan et al. (2021) (observed $I=|\hat k\times b|^{1+\sigma}\min(1/|\cos\zeta|,100)\,\mathcal P(r)\,g^{3+\sigma}$, fully polarized); PolarizedDisk is the AART g-disk with $|P|=I$ along the field direction fixed by a ZAMO-frame field.

The closed-form EVPA agrees with the numerical polarized march of this package, whose camera frame is parallel-transported along each RK4 step: the difference falls as $C^{-4}$ to a floor set by the plane-parallel camera, $2\times10^{-5}$ rad at a camera distance of $10^5\,M$ and $10^{-8}$ rad (median) at $10^9\,M$. It agrees with Krang's polarized synchrotron material and AART's polarization model to rounding error (NMSE of $I,Q,U$ between $10^{-28}$ and $10^{-24}$, EVPA medians of $10^{-15}$ rad), after two corrections to Krang (the Stokes $I$ weight of its polarized material and the emission polar sign on far-hemisphere cones) described in the validation record.

Sampling the meshblock hierarchy

An AthenaK snapshot holds cell averages on blocks of $n_1\times n_2\times n_3$ cells whose refinement levels differ by at most one between blocks that share a face, an edge or a corner. The sampled field in a block is the trilinear interpolant of its cells extended by one layer of ghost cells, built once when the snapshot is loaded (Data.fill_ghosts!), so a point between the outermost cell centre and the block face blends the outermost cell with the ghost cell beyond the face. The ghost at centre $\mathbf p$, with the block's cell widths $\mathbf h$, takes the value $V(\mathbf p,\mathbf h)$ of the mesh on that cell (Data.mesh_value):

  • a block of the same level covers $\mathbf p$: its cell (copy);
  • a finer block covers $\mathbf p$: the mean of $V$ over the children of the cell, the volume average of the finer cells inside it (restriction);
  • a coarser block, of cell widths $\mathbf H$, covers $\mathbf p$: from the coarse cell $c$ that contains $\mathbf p$,

    \[V=u_c+\sum_a\operatorname{minmod}\big(u_c-V(\mathbf x_c-H_a\hat{\mathbf e}_a),\; V(\mathbf x_c+H_a\hat{\mathbf e}_a)-u_c\big)\,\frac{p_a-x_{c,a}}{H_a},\]

    the limited piecewise-linear prolongation of AthenaK (ProlongCC), its neighbours taken at the coarse widths by the same rules (where the finer block covers one, its restriction);

  • \[\mathbf p\]

    lies outside the mesh along axis $a$: $V=2V(\mathbf p-sh_a\hat{\mathbf e}_a)-V(\mathbf p-2sh_a\hat{\mathbf e}_a)$, $s$ the outward sign, along each such axis in turn (one-sided linear extrapolation; the value $V(\mathbf p-sh_a\hat{\mathbf e}_a)$ is held where the mesh is one cell thick).

Continuity. A ghost value depends on its centre and widths alone, not on the block that asks. Two blocks of one level that share a face therefore blend the same eight nodes at every point of the face, and the sampled field is continuous across it to rounding, where the face's edges and corners border coarser or finer blocks too. Across a face between two levels the sides interpolate on different nodes and differ at second order: continuity there would need the fine side's trace on the face, bilinear between fine tangential nodes, to equal the coarse side's, bilinear between coarse ones, and their kinks do not coincide.

Order. Copy, restriction (the mean of a linear field over a cell is its centre value), the limited slope of linear data (the two one-sided differences are equal) and linear extrapolation are exact for a linear field, so a linear field is sampled exactly everywhere: the faces, edges and corners between levels and the outer boundary included. For a smooth field restriction and extrapolation err by $O(h^2)$. The limited slope departs from the derivative by $O(H)$ only where the one-sided differences change sign, within a cell of an extremum, where the derivative is itself $O(H)$; times the offset $|p_a-x_{c,a}|=H_a/4$ the prolonged value errs by $O(H^2)$. Trilinear interpolation of second-order nodes is second order, so the sampled field is second-order accurate everywhere (test/infra/data_ghosts.jl: a quadratic field on a three-level mesh converges at second order, the coarse–fine shells included).

Why the limiter. The unlimited slope $(u_{c+1}-u_{c-1})/2H$ is second order too and also a function of position, but at the offset $H/4$ it overshoots wherever the field changes by a large factor across a coarse cell (the torus surface, a funnel wall): $u=(1,1,100)$ prolongs to $-11.4$. The minmod slope keeps every prolonged value between $u_c$ and its neighbours; copy and restriction are averages; the trilinear blend is a convex combination of its nodes. Inside the mesh the sampled field therefore stays within the range of the cells around it: no new extrema, and $\rho>0$, $e>0$ wherever the cells have them. Only the extrapolated ghosts at the outer boundary can leave that range, and a non-positive density or pressure decodes as vacuum.

A block of $n^3$ cells is stored with $(n+2)^3$ (20% more memory for $32^3$, 10% for $64^3$); the ghost layers travel with the block data to every device, so threads and kernels run the same stencil. Imaging, the slow-light windows (each frame carries its own ghost layers), the uniform resample and the Monte Carlo march all sample this field; the Monte Carlo emission zones sit at cell centres, where the sample is the cell value.

The Z4c slices of the numerical-relativity solver are sampled by the same rule: Data.sample_var and the ADM resample Data.resample_z4c_adm blend, in the finest block that contains the point, its cells with the ghost layer beyond its faces, each ghost the value $V(\mathbf p,\mathbf h)$ of the dump's columns (Data.ghost_value on Data.BinColumns), evaluated where the stencil reaches it instead of stored. The nodes of the ADMGrid are then continuous across faces between blocks of one level and exact for a linear field everywhere, and inside a block they are the in-block trilinear samples. A node that held the outermost cell would put a whole cell step $h\,\partial_a f$ between the two nodes on either side of a face, a slope $h/\Delta$ times the field's for a node spacing $\Delta<h$, and the Catmull–Rom interpolant differentiates that slope into the Christoffel symbols: on the $192^3$ grid of the head-on slice ($\Delta=24/191\,M$, $h=1/4\,M$) the factor is $1.99$ in a slab about each face.

Radiative transfer

A single plasma-decode chain converts sampled code-unit $(\rho,p_{\rm gas},b^2)$ to cgs $(n_e,\Theta_e,B,\sigma,\beta^{-1})$, with the $R_{\rm high}/R_{\rm low}$ electron-temperature prescription (Mościbrodzka et al., 2016) and configurable $\sigma$, $\beta$, $\Theta_e$ emission cuts. The same chain feeds imaging and Monte Carlo.

Coefficients. coefficients returns the invariant emissivities, absorptivities, and rotativities $(j_{I,Q,V};\ \alpha_{I,Q,V};\ \rho_{Q,V})$ in the fluid frame, additively mixing three electron distribution functions:

  • thermal — polarized emissivities in the form of Dexter (2016), with the fits of Marszewski et al. (2021) for the Faraday rotation ($\rho_V$) and conversion ($\rho_Q$) coefficients, absorption by Kirchhoff's law;
  • power-law — Pandya et al. (2016) fits with host-precomputed $p$-dependent constants;
  • kappa — Pandya et al. (2016) low/high-frequency asymptotes joined by harmonic bridges, with precomputed $\Gamma$, Bessel, and ${}_2F_1$ factors.

All special functions expensive on device (Bessel $K_\nu$, $\Gamma$, hypergeometric) are either evaluated in host-side precomputation or replaced by device-safe polynomial fits, so the per-step kernel code is closed-form.

Unpolarized transport uses the exact constant-coefficient segment solution of the invariant transfer equation $d\mathcal I/d\lambda=j_{\rm inv}-\alpha_{\rm inv}\mathcal I$, written as the affine operator $\mathcal I\mapsto A\mathcal I+B$ with $A=e^{-\Delta\tau}$, $B=(j/\alpha)\,e^{-\Delta\tau}\,\mathrm{expm1}(\Delta\tau)$, and saturation to the source function when thick.

Polarized transport applies the exact solution of the transfer equation with constant coefficients per segment in the fluid tetrad (Gram–Schmidt frame anchored to $u^\mu$, $k^\mu$, $b^\mu$), as the affine operator $S\mapsto MS+b$, $M=e^{-\mathbf K\Delta s}$, $b=\int_0^{\Delta s}e^{-\mathbf K s}\,ds\;j$ (segment). With $\mathbf K=\alpha_I\mathbf 1+\mathbf N$, the eigenvalues of $\mathbf N$ are $\pm\lambda_1$ and $\pm i\lambda_2$, $\lambda_{1,2}^2=\big[(\alpha_P^2-\rho_P^2)^2/4+(\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol\rho)^2\big]^{1/2}\pm(\alpha_P^2-\rho_P^2)/2$, and Landi Degl'Innocenti and Landi Degl'Innocenti (1985)

\[e^{-\mathbf K s}=e^{-\alpha_I s}\Big[\tfrac12(\cosh\lambda_1 s+\cos\lambda_2 s)\,\mathbf M_1 -\sin\lambda_2 s\,\mathbf M_2-\sinh\lambda_1 s\,\mathbf M_3 +\tfrac12(\cosh\lambda_1 s-\cos\lambda_2 s)\,\mathbf M_4\Big],\]

so $b$ needs the four integrals $C_1\pm S_1=\Delta s\,\varphi\big((\alpha_I\mp\lambda_1)\Delta s\big)$ of $e^{-\alpha_I s}(\cosh,\sinh)\lambda_1 s$ and $C_2+iS_2=\Delta s\,\varphi\big((\alpha_I-i\lambda_2)\Delta s\big)$ of $e^{-\alpha_I s}(\cos,\sin)\lambda_2 s$, with $\varphi(z)=(1-e^{-z})/z$: $b=\big[\tfrac12(C_1+C_2)\mathbf M_1-S_2\mathbf M_2-S_1\mathbf M_3+\tfrac12(C_1-C_2)\mathbf M_4\big]j$. Their usual closed forms carry $1/(\alpha_I^2-\lambda_1^2)$ and $1/(\alpha_I^2+\lambda_2^2)$ and are differences of terms of order $1/(\alpha_I\mp\lambda_1)$ whose difference is of order $\Delta s$, so they keep only a relative accuracy $\epsilon/[(\alpha_I-\lambda_1)\Delta s]$ of their value ($\epsilon$ the rounding unit; the cosine pair fares better, $|\alpha_I-i\lambda_2|\ge\alpha_I-\lambda_1$). The segment uses them only where the slow-mode depth $(\alpha_I-\lambda_1)\Delta s\ge1$ and the relative gap to either pole exceeds $10^{-6}$, and evaluates $\varphi$, regular at $z=0$, everywhere else. That is the small-absorption limit: as $\alpha\to0$ at fixed $\rho$, $\lambda_1\to|\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol\rho|/\rho_P$, $\lambda_2\to\rho_P$, $C_1\to\Delta s$, $S_1\to0$ and $C_2+iS_2\to[\sin\theta+i(1-\cos\theta)]/\rho_P$ ($\theta=\rho_P\Delta s$), the rotation segment below, whereas the closed forms become $0/0$ (once $\alpha_I\lesssim10^{-154}$ their denominators underflow, as for the far gas of a power-law or kappa fit). The eigen-decomposition is formed scale-free: $\lambda_{1,2}$ are homogeneous of degree 1 in $(\boldsymbol\alpha_P,\boldsymbol\rho_P)$ and $\mathbf M_{2,3,4}$ of degree 0, so both come from the polarized coefficients divided by the largest of them, and of $\lambda_{1,2}^2$ the root that the formula gives as a difference is taken as $(\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol\rho)^2$ over the other ($\lambda_1^2\lambda_2^2=(\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\boldsymbol\rho)^2$). No square under- or overflows and neither root cancels; with $\rho_P=10^{10}\alpha_I$ the difference form loses $\lambda_1$ altogether and the slow mode decays at $\alpha_I$ instead of $\alpha_I-\lambda_1$. The cases:

  • emission only ($\alpha_I=\alpha_P=\rho_P=0$): $M=\mathbf 1$, $b=j\,\Delta s$;
  • total absorption ($\alpha_P=\rho_P=0$): $M=e^{-\alpha_I\Delta s}\mathbf 1$, $b=(1-e^{-\alpha_I\Delta s})\,j/\alpha_I$;
  • Faraday rotation and conversion without absorption ($\alpha_I=\alpha_P=0$): $e^{-\mathbf N s}$ rotates $(Q,U,V)$ about $\hat{\boldsymbol\rho}$ through $\theta=\rho_P s$, $e^{-\mathbf N s}=\cos\theta\,\mathbf 1+(1-\cos\theta)\,\hat{\boldsymbol\rho}\hat{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\mathsf T}+\sin\theta\,[\hat{\boldsymbol\rho}\times]$, and integrating each term over the segment ($\theta=\rho_P\Delta s$) gives $b_I=j_I\Delta s$ and $\boldsymbol b_P=\frac{\sin\theta}{\rho_P}\boldsymbol j_P+\frac{\theta-\sin\theta}{\rho_P}\hat{\boldsymbol\rho}(\hat{\boldsymbol\rho}\cdot\boldsymbol j_P)+\frac{1-\cos\theta}{\rho_P}\hat{\boldsymbol\rho}\times\boldsymbol j_P$;
  • the dichroism-cone boundary $|\alpha_P|=\alpha_I$ with $\rho=0$ (or $\boldsymbol\rho\parallel\boldsymbol\alpha$): $\lambda_1=\alpha_I$, the mode polarized against $\hat{\boldsymbol\alpha}$ is unabsorbed and gathers its emission linearly, $C_1-S_1=\Delta s$; the $\varphi$ form is this limit of the general solution;
  • nilpotent coupling ($\lambda_1=\lambda_2=0$, i.e. $|\alpha_P|=|\rho_P|$ and $\boldsymbol\alpha\perp\boldsymbol\rho$): $\mathbf N^4=0$, so $e^{-\mathbf K s}=e^{-\alpha_I s}(\mathbf 1-s\mathbf N+s^2\mathbf N^2/2-s^3\mathbf N^3/6)$ and $b=\sum_k(-\mathbf N)^k g_k\,j$, $g_k=\int_0^{\Delta s}s^k e^{-\alpha_I s}\,ds/k!$.

A segment is saturated ($M=0$, $b=\mathbf K^{-1}j$) when its slowest mode, at the rate $\alpha_I-\lambda_1$, is deeper than 100; a deeper segment with a shallower slow mode takes the $\varphi$ form, whose decay factors stay finite. Every case matches the 256-bit solution $\exp\!\big(\Delta s\,[\,-\mathbf K\ \ j;\ 0\ \ 0\,]\big)$ to rounding error (test/physics/transfer_segments.jl). Instead of transporting the coherency tensor $N^{\mu\nu}$ (Mościbrodzka and Gammie, 2018), the camera polarization frame (its vertical leg and 4-velocity) is parallel-transported along the ray with the same fourth-order Runge–Kutta step as the geodesic, and each segment operator is rotated into it by a Q–U rotation $R$ (the angle between the fluid $e_{(1)}$ and the transported screen leg). The Stokes vector is accumulated camera-to-source with a running Mueller transmission $G$: $S\mathrel{+}=G\,(Rb)$, $G\leftarrow G\,(RMR^{\mathsf T})$. Per-ray state is $O(1)$ regardless of path length, and the operator of each segment is exact for constant coefficients, so the transfer adds no discretisation error beyond the sampling of the coefficients along the ray. On a Kerr torus deck ($a=0.9375$, $16^2$ pixels, $C=20$–$320$ against $C=1280$) the scheme converges at first order in $1/C$ like the coefficient quadrature, with 30% smaller Stokes errors and five to eight times smaller EVPA errors than a Strang-split coherency transport at equal $C$ ($C=80$: $3.75\times10^{-3}$ / $0.008^\circ$ against $5.40\times10^{-3}$ / $0.065^\circ$); its Stokes $I$ departs from the unpolarized march by $1.9\times10^{-7}$ at $C=1280$, against $1.1\times10^{-4}$ for the coherency transport. fluid_tetrad_checked ends a march whose screen leg has no spacelike solution ($k$ far off the null cone at a coarse step) instead of producing a NaN.

In the fluid frame $e_{(2)}$ is the projection of $b^\mu$, the basis of the Marszewski et al. (2021) coefficients ($Q>0$ in the $k$–$B$ plane); the tetrad rule applied at the camera gives the IEEE/IAU output of Conventions with no relabelling.

Admissibility. In production every segment operator projects its coefficients onto the physical cone before it is formed: when $|\alpha_P|=(\alpha_Q^2+\alpha_V^2)^{1/2}$ exceeds $\alpha_I$ the polarized absorptivities are rescaled onto $|\alpha_P|=\alpha_I$ (onto $\alpha_P=0$ when $\alpha_I=0$), and when $|j_P|$ exceeds $j_I$ the polarized emissivities are rescaled onto $|j_P|=j_I$, keeping $j_I$ and the polarization direction. segment(...; project = false) solves the transfer equation for the coefficients as given, outside the cone too; the one-zone tests of Prather et al. (2023), whose coefficients lie outside it, run through that path. The thermal fits of Marszewski et al. (2021) give $|j_P|>j_I$ at small pitch angles; without the rescale the observed Stokes vector would depend on whether such an emitter lies in front of or behind brighter gas, because the accumulated vector is clamped to $|P|\le I$ (stokes_admissible) only on partial sums (test/physics/transfer_segments.jl: a thermal slab and a field-aligned screen give the same $V/I$ in either order).

One physics source. The plasma decode, the coefficient functions (fed by the isbits parameter bundle TransferParams), the fluid tetrad, the segment operators and the reverse march (march, in the mode StokesI or IQUV) are single functions called by the threaded executor and by the device kernel. A march control supplies the geodesic: SpacetimeMarch (the fixed-control march on any spacetime, Float64 or Float32 lane) or StoredPath (the states of trace). The two backends run the same program, so on identical trajectories they agree to the floating-point contraction of the two targets.

One kernel, two backends

Every product is a per-item program, one item per pixel, block or primary photon, run by an executor: ThreadedExecutor calls it from Julia threads, PoolExecutor runs it through the single kernel of Execution on the lanes of a DevicePool. The program is the same code on both: the reverse march of Radiative transfer advances the geodesic, the two transported frame vectors and the Stokes accumulation in one loop, storing no trajectory, so per-ray state is $O(1)$ regardless of path length. Metric derivatives come from ForwardDiff dual numbers evaluated inside the march; no Christoffel tensor is materialized. The same mechanism seeds a dual on a physical scalar (the density mass scale, $R_{\rm high}$) through the transfer accumulation, so sensitivity returns the image and its exact parameter derivative in one pass (the differentiable-GRRT construction of Jipole (Naethe Motta et al., 2025)). The fluid is sampled on the AthenaK meshblock hierarchy on both backends with the same trilinear stencil on ghost-extended blocks (Sampling the meshblock hierarchy); a UniformGrid resample is the cheaper alternative, at the percent level in flux and with EVPA changes on Faraday-thick decks.

Multi-device execution

Every device run uses a persistent DevicePool, one per device list, created on first use: two worker tasks per device, each with its own stream, device buffers and pinned staging buffers. Work is a queue of chunks; each worker pulls the next chunk when it finishes the last, so a slower or shared device takes fewer. The design rules:

  • Ownership, not partition, fixes the result. Every ray writes only its own pixel, every AMR block only its own tile, and every Monte Carlo photon draws from a stream named by its genealogy, not by the chunk that carries it. The output is therefore bitwise identical for any device count, chunk size and schedule (test/pipeline/products_backend.jl, test/infra/execution_pool.jl).
  • Rays are generated on the device. A chunk uploads only its pixel or block indices (4 bytes per item); the device builds each camera ray with the same camera_ray function as the host (test/infra/execution_ray_sources.jl: device rays isequal host rays).
  • Longest work first. On Kerr, prepare queues $16\times16$-pixel tiles in order of predicted cost — distance to the critical curve (cost_order) — so the rays that wind near the photon ring start first instead of ending the render; march_sort_order(counts) orders by measured trip counts from a previous frame, longest first, through the queue keyword of prepare. With MixedLanes the near-ring pixels that need Float64 enter the same queue ahead of the Float32 pixels (test/infra/execution_schedule.jl).
  • Streaming adaptive refinement. Each refinement block is a work item; the worker that receives a block's Stokes values paints it, runs the refinement test and queues its four children at once, so refined blocks march while the rest of their parent level is in flight. Mosaic, level map and ray count are independent of the executor and the completion order (test/pipeline/products_amr.jl).
  • Upload once. A snapshot or uniform grid is uploaded once per pool and reused by later calls on the same object; Monte Carlo host tables are built once per call and uploaded to all devices concurrently.

The pool needs at least one more Julia thread than devices (-t 3 for two GPUs) so that the workers of different devices run concurrently. On the KernelAbstractions CPU backend, HostDevice entries act as logical devices, which is how the invariance tests run without a GPU. The measured throughput on one and two A100s is on Benchmarks; the execution model and its knobs are in Performance.

Adaptive image-plane refinement

render_amr implements the adaptive ray tracing of White (2022): the image is tiled into blocks; the indicators of Analysis (relative gradient, relative Laplacian, absolute gradient, or any callable) flag blocks, which spawn four children at doubled pixel density up to max_level. All levels run the plan's imaging program; the blocks stream through the executor's worklist (Multi-device execution), and both backends make identical refinement decisions. Refinement typically saves $\sim4\times$ in rays over a uniform image at the finest pixel.

Monte Carlo spectral synthesis

The Monte Carlo module implements the grmonty scheme (Dolence et al., 2009): superphoton packets sample the synchrotron emissivity zone-by-zone with frequency and pitch-angle rejection sampling and a log-frequency weight table, in an emission tetrad whose first spatial vector is the field and whose other two come from the coordinate axes by Gram–Schmidt, an axis nearly parallel to the vectors before it giving way to the best-conditioned one (a field along $x^3$ would otherwise leave no direction for the last vector); packets carry covariant momentum and decay by Kirchhoff absorption along geodesics. Compton scattering uses the thermally averaged ("hot") Klein–Nishina cross section tabulated on a log-log $(\varepsilon,\Theta_e)$ grid, scattering electrons drawn from the relativistic Maxwellian by the Canfield et al. (1987) sampler and exact Klein–Nishina/Thomson rest-frame kinematics; the scattering events of a step follow the biased exponential transform Kahn (1950) in the exact form below. Spectra are binned in (exit angle, $\log\nu$) with grmonty normalization; the optically thin synchrotron sphere matches its closed form within its per-bin standard errors, and GRMHD spectra show the gravitationally redshifted synchrotron bump. On GPU, multi-order scattering is layered rather than recursive: the host relaunches once per scattering generation, and a launch whose child buffer overflows is rerun at the exact size, so no child is dropped. The march reads any spacetime through proxy_distance: the distance above the horizon proxy sets the step (grmonty's stepsize) and the capture, the radius the escape, so the same tracker runs on Kerr, on a binary at rest or in motion (the nearest hole at the photon's time) and on an ADM grid.

The angle-integrated emissivity. With $X=\nu/\nu_s$, $\nu_s=\tfrac29\nu_c\Theta_e^2\sin\theta$, $\nu_c=eB/2\pi m_ec$, the thermal fit of thermal_synchrotron is $j_\nu=(n_ee^2\nu_c/c)(\sqrt2\pi/27)\sin\theta\,G(X)$ with $G(X)=(X^{1/2}+2^{11/12}X^{1/6})^2e^{-X^{1/3}}$. It depends on $\nu$ and $\Theta_e$ only through $K=\nu/(\tfrac29\nu_c\Theta_e^2)=X\sin\theta$, so

\[\int j_\nu\,d\Omega=4\pi\!\int_0^{\pi/2}\! j_\nu\sin\theta\,d\theta =\frac{4\pi n_e e^2\nu_c}{c}\,\frac{\sqrt2\,\pi}{27}\,F(K),\qquad F(K)=\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin^2\theta\;G\!\left(\frac{K}{\sin\theta}\right)d\theta ,\]

exactly. EmissivityTable holds $\ln F$ and $d\ln F/d\ln K$ (the derivative under the integral) on 6,324 nodes, $\Delta\ln K\le0.02$ from $K=10^{-30}$ to $10^3$ and $\le0.005$ from $10^3$ to $3\times10^8$ (where the curvature of $\ln F$ grows as $K^{1/3}$), from a 2,048-interval Simpson rule; int_jnu interpolates by cubic Hermite polynomials, continues $\ln F$ along the first slope below the table ($F\propto K^{1/3}$) and returns 0 above it ($F<10^{-280}$). The table agrees with the 512-interval rule of int_jnu_simpson to $3\times10^{-9}$ and with an 8,192-interval rule to $7\times10^{-11}$ over the whole range, and costs one logarithm and one exponential per evaluation. It is built once, when the package compiles, and generation reads the same function as the zone tables, so the frequency acceptance and its envelope stay consistent.

Zones, weights and counts. The number of emission events in a spacetime region is a scalar and the fluid-frame element $dV_f\,d\tau_f$ is the invariant $\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$, so a zone of measure $dV=\sqrt{-g}\,\Delta^3x\,L^3$ emits $d\dot N/d\ln\nu=(dV/h)\int j_\nu d\Omega$ photons per second of coordinate time per unit $\ln\nu$ (the fluid's own volume $u^t\sqrt{-g}\,\Delta^3x$ and proper time $dt/u^t$ carry $u^t$ factors that cancel). The weight table $w_m=\sum_z\int j_\nu d\Omega(\nu_m)\,dV_z/(hN_s')$ on $n+1$ nodes over $[\nu_{\min},\nu_{\max}]$, $N_s'=N_s/\ln(\nu_{\max}/\nu_{\min})$, makes the generated density uniform in $\ln\nu$; zone $z$ receives $N_z=(dV_z/h)\,\Delta\ln\nu\sum_m c_m\int j_\nu d\Omega(\nu_m)/w_m$ primaries (rounded stochastically), with the trapezoid weights $c_0=c_n=\tfrac12$, $c_m=1$ inside. Then $\sum_zN_z=N_s'\Delta\ln\nu\sum_mc_m=N_s$: the expected number of primaries is the target, and the emitted power carries no excess of one end node. Both passes over the zones (the exact node sums of the weight table, then the counts) stream: the zone × frequency matrix is never stored. On a block mesh every cell centre that its block owns is a zone. A zone below the horizon proxy is dropped, since a photon emitted there is captured before its first step; this leaves every escaping estimate unchanged. For quick runs stride = s cuts each block into groups of $s^3$ cells and draws one of a group's $n_\mathrm{own}$ owned cells uniformly, on a stream of the seed, with the volume $n_\mathrm{own}\,\Delta V$ of the drawn cell: for any $f$, $\mathrm E\big[n_\mathrm{own}f(c)\Delta V(c)\big]=\sum_{c\in\text{owned}}f(c)\Delta V(c)$, so the strided zones are an unbiased quadrature of the stride-1 sum (a fixed cell per group is not: where the emissivity changes by decades within a group it moves the emission between radii).

Scattering within a step. Over a step of absorption depth $a=\Delta\tau_a$ and scattering depth $s=\Delta\tau_s$ (trapezoid, rates uniform in the fraction $f$ of the step), $\Delta\tau=a+s$, a photon of weight $w$ scatters at $f$ with expected weight density $p(f)=w\,s\,e^{-\Delta\tau f}$ and leaves with $w\,e^{-\Delta\tau}$. The events of the step are a Poisson process of mean $bs$ for a bias $b\ge1$, at fractions drawn independently from $q(f)=\Delta\tau e^{-\Delta\tau f}/(1-e^{-\Delta\tau})$, each spawning a child of weight $W=w(1-e^{-\Delta\tau})/(b\Delta\tau)$. The expected child weight density is

\[b s\cdot W\cdot q(f)=w\,s\,e^{-\Delta\tau f}=p(f)\]

for every $b$, $a$ and $s$, and $W\le w/b$. The parent decays deterministically by $e^{-\Delta\tau}$. The events are drawn as the points of a rate-$bs$ Poisson process on the CDF coordinate $u=(1-e^{-\Delta\tau f})/(1-e^{-\Delta\tau})\in[0,1)$, cumulated exponential gaps on the photon's stream, so a step without an event costs one uniform. The ledger of a march records, per step, the scattered weight $w s(1-e^{-\Delta\tau})/\Delta\tau$ (the expectation of the spawned weight) and the absorbed power $wEa(1-e^{-\Delta\tau})/\Delta\tau$; their sum with the final weight closes the photon's budget exactly. An event whose site is outside the plasma spawns nothing and its weight is counted (the trapezoid depth of a step that leaves the plasma), as is the scattered weight of the order max_scatt, which may not spawn.

The importance bias. Let $b$ be any positive function of what is known before the step's draws: the photon (weight, energy, order, history) and the step's samples. Conditioned on that state, the expected child density is $p(f)$ whatever $b$, and the parent's weight does not depend on $b$ at all; by the tower property every expectation linear in the escaping photons, each bin of $\nu L_\nu$ included, is independent of the rule. The default StateBias is $b=\mathrm{clamp}(b_0A(\Theta_e)\,(w/w_\mathrm{ref})\,f^{\,n},1,b_\mathrm{max})$ for a photon scattered $n$ times, with $A(\Theta_e)=1+4\Theta_e+16\Theta_e^2$ (the cold and hot limits of the mean energy gain $1+4\Theta_eK_3/K_2$ of one Thomson scattering, at most 15 % above it), $\Theta_e$ the larger end temperature of the step, $w_\mathrm{ref}$ the mean primary weight and $f\ge1$ the factor per scattering order (bias_order, 1 by default). A primary's child then carries about $w_\mathrm{ref}/(b_0A)$ photons and the energy weight $w_\mathrm{ref}E/b_0$ whatever the gas that scattered it, so the rare scatterings of heavy seed photons off hot electrons, which feed the highest frequencies, are split into many light children rather than a few heavy ones; $b_\mathrm{max}$ bounds the events per step. That child's own bias is about $fA'/A$, near 1 for $f=1$, so with $f=1$ the light scattered two and three times, which makes the highest frequencies of an optically thick corona, is sampled little; $f>1$ raises the bias of every later generation by $f$ per scattering. With split $>1$, an event whose uncapped bias exceeds $b_\mathrm{max}$ spawns $m$ children of weight $W/m$ with independent kicks, which keeps the expected child density by linearity.

The estimator and its variance. A bin collects $\nu L_\nu=(m_ec^2/\Delta\ln\nu)(4\pi/\Delta\Omega)\sum_jw_jE_j$. Its variance is estimated by $(m_ec^2/\Delta\ln\nu)^2(4\pi/\Delta\Omega)^2\sum_j(w_jE_j)^2$, the variance of a compound Poisson sum, accumulated exactly beside $\sum wE$ (nuLnu_sigma); the effective number of photons $N_\mathrm{eff}=(\sum wE)^2/\sum(wE)^2$ sets the relative error $N_\mathrm{eff}^{-1/2}$. Children of one parent that land in one bin are correlated, which this photon-level estimate does not see; seed-to-seed scatter checks it.

Photons certain to be captured. On Kerr, the radial potential of a photon moving inward inside every spherical photon orbit has no root above the horizon (Teo, 2003; Gralla and Lupsasca, 2020), so a photon that may spawn no more children stops, captured, once a step moves it inward below the prograde equatorial photon orbit less 0.02 M. No photon that would escape is stopped, and the bins are bitwise those of the full march.

Streams keyed by ancestry. Randomness is the counter-based Philox4x32-10 generator (Salmon et al., 2011). Each photon's stream is named by its genealogy: a 128-bit identity $I=g+\sum_{j=1}^{k}e_j\,2^{G+(j-1)E}+k\,2^{124}$, with $g<2^G$ the primary's global index, $e_j<2^E$ the ordinal of the scattering event of the layer-$j$ ancestor along its parent's march, and $k\le15$ the scattering layer, carried in Philox key word 2 and counter words 2–4 (the draw index in counter word 1, the seed in key word 1). widest_layout sizes $G$ from the primary count and gives the event field the widest width that fits, $E=\min(32,\lfloor(124-G)/k_{\max}\rfloor)$; an event beyond it spawns nothing and is counted. Distinct genealogies map to disjoint bit fields and hence distinct Philox inputs for every draw; identities with bit 123 set and $k=0$ name the host streams of a run (the cell drawn in a strided zone). A child's kick and its march draw from its own stream, so the parent's stream carries only its own events. A primary with $g<2^{32}$ has the stream MCRng(seed, g). No stream depends on the thread, chunk, device or buffer slot that carries the photon.

Exact accumulation. Spectrum bins and scattering diagnostics are summed exactly. Every finite double is an integer multiple of $2^{-1074}$ below $2^{1024}$; the accumulator stores that integer in 68 signed 64-bit limbs of 31 bits (2108 bits for the 2098-bit range). One addend touches three consecutive limbs, each by less than $2^{31}$, so a bin holds at least $2^{32}-1$ addends before a limb can overflow, and the sum is rounded once to nearest-even at the end. Integer addition is associative, so the result is independent of order, grouping and atomic interleaving; the cost is three integer adds (atomic on a device) per addend. A fixed-point window of two or three words would not suffice: the synchrotron weight table alone spans about $2^{152}$.

With both, spectra, diagnostics and the set of scattered children are bitwise identical for any thread count, device count and schedule (test/physics/numerics_exactsum.jl, test/pipeline/products_montecarlo.jl, test/pipeline/products_backend.jl), and the device kernel on the KernelAbstractions CPU backend reproduces the threaded run bit for bit.

Emission over a window and arrival time

With an emission window $[t_a,t_b]$ of coordinate time the plasma changes while the superphotons travel. The emission rate density is a sum of nodes, $\dot n(t)=\sum_m\phi_m(t)\,\dot n_m$, each $\dot n_m$ the complete stationary emission of one plasma state and $\phi_m\ge0$ a profile in time. A frozen snapshot or sphere is one node with $\phi=1$ on the window; a series of frames at $\tau_k$ gives each frame the hat that is 1 at $\tau_k$ and 0 at its neighbours, so $\sum_k\phi_k\equiv1$ and the emission is the linear interpolation in time of the frames' emission, and a run of equal frames is one node with the sum of its hats. (The emissivity of linearly blended primitives is not linear in $t$; the two interpolants of the same frames differ at second order in their spacing, as the cell-centre emission and the interpolated absorption do in space.) The node's share of the window's emission is $W_m=\int\phi_m\,dt/(t_b-t_a)$, and the window mean of the emission is the stationary emission of the nodes' zones with the measure $W_m\,dV$: one weight table and the counts of the stationary scheme over the concatenated zones give the expected number of primaries $N_s$ and one weight $w(\nu)$ for every packet, which is now the window mean of the rate, so a packet stands for $w\,(t_b-t_a)\,L/c$ photons. A primary's emission time is drawn exactly from its node's piecewise-linear profile (a segment by its area; on it the density $\propto(1-s)v_0+sv_1$ as the mixture of $2(1-s)$ and $2s$ with weights $v_0$, $v_1$), on its own identity's Philox stream from draw $2^{31}$, so the stationary draws are untouched.

Each primary is the stationary photon of its node, generated at coordinate time 0, carried to its emission time: by the time translation on a stationary spacetime, and for a plasma co-rotating at $\omega$ also by the rotation $R_z(\omega t_e)$ of its position and of the spatial components of its covariant momentum. The second is exact when the spacetime has the helical Killing vector $\partial_t+\omega\partial_\phi$, $g(Rx,t+\tau)=R\,g(x,t)R^{\mathsf T}$: the photon is null at its event and the zone's $\sqrt{-g}$, $n_e$, $\Theta_e$ and $b^2$ are invariants of the map. Kerr with its spin along $z$ and flat space have it at every $\omega$, two holes with spins along $z$ on a circular orbit at the orbital frequency; a frozen snapshot or a series emits over a window only on a stationary spacetime. The march reads the metric and the plasma at the photon's $x^0$: frames blended linearly in their primitives, the co-rotating plasma at $R_z(-\omega x^0)x$ with its vectors rotated back, the variable sphere at its density of the time. A scattered child starts with its event's time.

Arrival time. A photon escaping at $(t_e,\mathbf x_e)$ along $\hat n=\mathbf k/|\mathbf k|$ crosses the wavefront $\hat n\cdot\mathbf x=D$ of a distant observer along $\hat n$ at $t_e+D-\hat n\cdot\mathbf x_e$ in flat space, so $t_{\rm arr}=t_e-\hat n\cdot\mathbf x_e$ up to a constant common to all photons; it is constant along a straight line, so it does not depend on where the march stops. Beyond the stop an outgoing ray of Kerr–Schild time keeps $t-r-4M\ln(r-2M)$ constant, so the constant is $D+4M\ln D$ and the photon-dependent remainder $-4M\ln r_e$ varies by at most $4M\ln(1+1/C)\approx4M/C$ among the photons stopped at one radius (the stop overshoots by about $r/C$), while the bending left beyond the stop moves $\hat n\cdot\mathbf x_e$ by about $2Mb^2/r_e^2$ for impact parameter $b$, at most of order $2M$: far below the arrival bins of an orbit. In flat space the arrival time is exact.

Light curves. Each photon is assigned to the observer along its own direction. An arrival bin of width $\Delta t$ in a row of solid angle $\Delta\Omega$ gives the isotropic-equivalent

\[\nu L_\nu=\frac{m_ec^2}{\Delta\ln\nu}\,\frac{4\pi}{\Delta\Omega}\,\frac{t_b-t_a}{\Delta t}\sum_{\rm bin}wE ,\]

with the standard error from $\sum(wE)^2$ as for the stationary bins; $L/c$ cancels. Summed over the bins with the weights $\Delta t/(t_b-t_a)$ it is the stationary estimator, the window mean. Arrival times within the march's emission radius $R$ of the window edges see part of the source (on flat space exactly those within the sphere radius). Without scattering each history adds at most one photon to a bin and $\sum(wE)^2$ overestimates the variance only by $\sum(\mathrm E\,X_i)^2$, a relative excess of the order of the largest probability that one history lands in the bin; children of one history in one bin add cross terms it does not see.

The frozen limit. A run with one node whose profile is flat on the window has $W=1$ exactly, so its tables, counts, streams and photons are the stationary run's but for $x^0$; on a stationary spacetime with time-independent matter nothing in the march reads $x^0$, the generation runs as one compiled function for both programs (its dot products are @simd reductions, whose rounding follows the inlining context) and the march runs in the stationary sink, the arrival time being recorded after it returns. The window-mean spectra, the ledger and the children are then the stationary ones bit for bit.

Exact tests. A uniform thin ball of radius $R$ seen along $\hat z$ has the delay distribution $P(\tau)=3(R^2-\tau^2)/4R^3$, so an emission $1+A\sin\omega t$ is observed as $1+A\hat P\sin\omega t$ with $\hat P=3(\sin x-x\cos x)/x^3=3j_1(x)/x$, $x=\omega R$, and no phase shift; a density interpolated linearly through $n$ nodes per period has the fundamental $A\,\mathrm{sinc}^2(\pi/n)$, so the light curve's is $A\,\mathrm{sinc}^2(\pi/n)\hat P$ exactly. A flash at the centre of a thin shell of radius $r$ and Thomson depth $\tau$ returns, to first order in $\tau$, the echo $\tau\,\Psi(t)$ with $\Psi=(3/8r)[1+(1-t/r)^2]$ on $0\le t\le2r$, averaged over the shell's depth profile. A co-rotating plasma on the orbiting binary obeys $L(t+\tau,\theta,\phi+\omega\tau)=L(t,\theta,\phi)$: azimuth-integrated light curves are flat, and at fixed $(\theta,\phi)$ the period is $2\pi/\omega$, halved only when the plasma, like the equal-mass metric, is symmetric under the rotation by $\pi$ (test/pipeline/products_montecarlo_time.jl).

Deviations from reference codes

Four deliberate, oracle-verified departures from Blacklight (White, 2022) and ipole (Mościbrodzka and Gammie, 2018):

  1. No dedicated $\rho=0$ coupling branch. Blacklight's absorption-without-rotation step, which it also applies to both halves of its split step, damps the emission terms of $I$ at the polarized rate and carries a sign error in one polarized term (reported to the developers together with the misplaced $(U,V)$ entries of its $\mathbf M_2$, $\mathbf M_3$). BlackLightPlus routes $\rho_P=0$ through the general solution above, whose limit it is.
  2. Transported camera frame instead of the coherency tensor. ipole parallel-transports the coherency tensor $N^{\mu\nu}$ between segments; BlackLightPlus transports the camera readout frame and applies the exact segment operator rotated into it (Radiative transfer). Against a Strang-split coherency transport at equal step density the EVPA error is five to eight times smaller.
  3. Degenerate eigenstructures take their exact limits (the dichroism-cone boundary, nilpotent coupling, rotation without absorption) in place of an approximate fallback.
  4. Interpolation across meshblock faces. Blacklight interpolates within a block and extrapolates linearly from the two outermost cells in the outer half cell (its default); with simulation_block_interp it takes the anchors beyond a face from the neighbouring blocks, but on the high side of each axis it forms the blend fraction from x1v(b, i + 1) one past the block, the first cell centre of the next block in storage order. BlackLightPlus interpolates across every face through its ghost layers (Sampling the meshblock hierarchy). On the AthenaK torus deck of the GRMHD-snapshot comparison ($i=163^\circ$, 230 GHz) Blacklight with that coordinate taken at the face, $2x^1_f(b,i+1)-x^1_v(b,i)$, agrees with BlackLightPlus to NMSE $9.7\times10^{-6}$, $2.1\times10^{-5}$, $2.1\times10^{-5}$, $5.5\times10^{-5}$ in $I$, $Q$, $U$, $V$ (net linear polarization 3.352% and 3.349%); its in-block extrapolation differs by $3.8\times10^{-4}$ in $I$ (3.246%).

The images are cross-validated against RAPTOR (Bronzwaer et al., 2018) on analytic scenes and binary-black-hole GRMHD snapshots, and against Blacklight (White, 2022) on a single-hole torus; protocol and results are on Validation.
