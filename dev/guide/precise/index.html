Precise ray tracing

The imaging products march every ray with a fixed-control step: the step is the distance to the horizon over a divisor C, and the classical fourth-order Runge–Kutta step (:rk4) advances the state. The precise mode replaces the integrator, the camera and the stopping rule so that the marched geodesics reach the accuracy of the closed-form Kerr tracer, on any spacetime the march supports.

using BlackLightPlus
using BlackLightPlus.Plans: precise
using BlackLightPlus.Products: render_crossings

a = 0.94
cfg = precise(RunConfig(camera = CameraConfig(inclination_deg = 17.0, fov = 50.0,
                                              resolution = 512)))
plan = prepare(cfg; spacetime = KerrSchildCartesian(a), matter = nothing)
disk = EquatorialDisk(a)

marched = render_crossings(plan, disk)            # the numerical march, orders 0, 1, 2
exact = render_analytic(plan, disk; ro = 1e15)    # the closed form, observer at infinity

The two images agree to rounding error: marched.Ib[n] and exact.Ib[n] differ by about 10⁻¹⁶ of the image maximum in the median pixel of order n = 0, 10⁻¹⁴ for n = 1 and 10⁻¹³ for n = 2, and by 10⁻¹² at the 99th percentile of n = 2, where the photon ring amplifies rounding most.

What the precise mode sets

precise(cfg) returns cfg with

max_steps2500at least 10⁵
  • :verner9 is the ninth-order step of Verner's 9(8) pair: fifteen right-hand sides per step against four, with an error that falls as C⁻⁹ instead of C⁻⁴. At equal cost it is many orders of magnitude more accurate; at C = 20 it reaches rounding error on Kerr.
  • winding shortens the step near a spinning horizon, where a backward-traced ray winds around the spin axis faster than its radius changes.
  • capture_redshift is the photon energy in the local normal frame at which a ray falling into a black hole is taken as captured; anything it would still meet is redshifted by at least that factor. At 10⁶ the omitted emission of a thin disk is below 10⁻¹⁵ of the image maximum.
  • camera.infinity makes the image coordinates the Bardeen coordinates of an observer at infinity, as in the closed-form tracer: each ray is carried in from infinity through the far zone of the metric and started on the plane at camera.distance. It needs a stationary spacetime (Kerr, a static binary, an ADM slice); a time-dependent one keeps the camera at its finite distance, precise(cfg; infinity = false). On a binary the redshift cut stays at 100 whatever the configuration: the horizons of a superposition are approximate, and a ray followed beyond that energy stalls above them and is lost.

When to use it

The precise mode serves the products whose accuracy is that of the geodesics: images of emission surfaces (render_crossings), trajectories (trace), shadows and capture maps (shadow), and the charges of rays on dynamical spacetimes. A volumetric render (render with a plasma) samples its plasma once per step, so its accuracy is set by the step divisor and not by the integrator (the flux of a torus image converges as C⁻² with either integrator): keep C at the production value or above, precise(cfg; C = 80.0).

The precise mode takes fewer, more expensive steps than the production march and spends none between the camera and the black hole, which the far zone covers in about twenty steps. On the CPU it costs 1.3 to 2 times the production march per ray at moderate spin and up to six times at spin 0.99, where every captured ray carries the winding-resolved step down to the redshift cut; precise(cfg; C = 14.0) reaches the same accuracy at about 70 % of the cost on both configurations of the code comparison.

Integrators and options one at a time

Every field can be set on its own. The production integrator converges at fourth order once the camera is at infinity:

cfg = RunConfig(camera = CameraConfig(inclination_deg = 17.0, infinity = true),
                C = 320.0, capture_redshift = 1e6, max_steps = 10^5)

With a redshift cut far above the production value near a fast-spinning black hole, set winding = true as well: an unresolved step near the horizon meets the equator at spurious points, whose intensities come out negative or not finite.

The ninth-order step can march a time-dependent spacetime with the camera at its finite distance:

cfg = precise(RunConfig(camera = CameraConfig(distance = 1000.0)); infinity = false)
plan = prepare(cfg; spacetime = BoostedSuperposedKerrSchild(masses, spins, orbit),
               matter = nothing)
tr = trace(plan)

The step functions are reachable directly through BlackLightPlus.Spacetime: march_step(Verner9(), st, y, h), surface_crossing(Verner9(), st, y, h, EquatorialPlane()), and infinity_ray(st, frame, α, β).

A user metric at infinity

The camera at infinity needs the offset (g^{μν} − η^{μν}) p_ν of the raised covector without the cancellation of subtracting η from g^{-1}. Kerr–Schild spacetimes provide it. A UserMetric is not stationary by declaration and keeps the finite camera; a stationary user spacetime type gains the camera at infinity by defining Spacetime.raise_index_offset(st, x, p) in closed form.
